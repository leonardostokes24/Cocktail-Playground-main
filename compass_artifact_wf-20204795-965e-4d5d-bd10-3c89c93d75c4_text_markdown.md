# Research Report: Building "Proof" — A Node-Based Cocktail R&D App and Open Spec Commons

## TL;DR
- **The market gap is real and defensible.** No existing bar-industry product combines visual, branching version-control of drink specs with cross-creator forking and preserved attribution — inventory/costing tools (Backbar, WISK, Partender) treat recipes as static cost objects, and social/reference databases (Difford's Guide, Kindred Cocktails, Untappd) offer flat recipe lists with no lineage. "GitHub × Untappd for cocktail specs" occupies genuinely open territory.
- **The technical stack is well-supported but has three known traps to design around:** React Flow re-render storms (solved with memoized nodes + Zustand selectors + `onlyRenderVisibleElements`), Postgres lineage/immutability (solved with adjacency-list `parent_id` + recursive CTEs + append-only snapshot tables), and glassmorphism accessibility/performance costs (solved by restricting `backdrop-filter` to small chrome surfaces with solid fallbacks).
- **The UK costing model must be built ex-VAT with 20% VAT and GP% (not US pour cost) as the primary metric.** Recipes are not copyrightable (facts), so CC-BY on the public commons is legally sound and culturally aligned with bartending's riff-and-credit norms.

## Key Findings

### Track 1 — Competitive Landscape
1. **Two separate software worlds exist, and neither does lineage.** Bar *operations* software (inventory + costing) and cocktail *reference/social* apps are distinct categories. Costing tools include recipe builders but frame them as cost/variance objects, not creative artifacts. Reference/social apps store flat recipes with no version history or forking.
2. **No competitor offers visual version-control or cross-creator forking with attribution.** The closest analogues are open-source hobby projects (Forkful, dishout.recipes, nextcloud/cookbook version-request issues) — none is a polished, bartender-facing product, and the cocktail-specific space is empty.
3. **Recipes are not copyrightable** (treated as "facts" in US/UK law); only names (trademark), photos/prose (copyright), and undisclosed formulas (trade secret) are protectable. This makes a CC-BY commons legally clean and matches the community's existing riff-and-credit culture.
4. **Costing is a top bartender pain point** currently handled in spreadsheets, and existing tools are criticized for being inventory-first, not creation-first.

### Track 2 — Technical Best Practices
5. **React Flow:** memoize custom nodes with `React.memo`, memoize handlers with `useCallback`, subscribe to Zustand slices with `useShallow`/selectors (never the whole nodes array), and enable `onlyRenderVisibleElements` for large graphs. React Flow uses Zustand internally, so a Zustand store is the recommended state layer.
6. **Supabase/Postgres:** enable RLS on every public-schema table (deny-by-default); use `SECURITY DEFINER` helper functions to avoid recursive-policy 500 errors and per-row subquery cost; model fork lineage as an adjacency list (`parent_id`) traversed with `WITH RECURSIVE` (with PG15 `SEARCH`/`CYCLE` for loop safety); store published specs as full immutable JSONB snapshots in an append-only versions table; use generated `tsvector` columns + GIN indexes with `websearch_to_tsquery` for search.
7. **Radial menus** are faster than linear menus for expert/muscle-memory use and suit touch, but are harder to learn; anchor them on the touched object, use a long-press trigger, cap at 6–8 slices, and offer accessible fallbacks.
8. **Glassmorphism/Liquid Glass** is the dominant 2025–2026 aesthetic but carries real accessibility (contrast/legibility) and GPU-performance costs; restrict `backdrop-filter` to small floating chrome, keep blur modest, respect reduce-transparency settings, and provide solid fallbacks.
9. **Money handling:** never use JS floats; use `decimal.js` and Postgres `numeric`, and note the `pg` driver returns numeric as strings to preserve precision.

### Track 3 — UK On-Trade Costing
10. **GP% is the UK metric** (not US pour cost); GP is calculated on the **ex-VAT** net selling price, with VAT at the standard 20% rate (unchanged for hospitality in 2025–2026 after the pandemic-era reductions expired).
11. **Wet GP targets** commonly cited around 70–75%+ for cocktail bars, with benchmark ranges varying by source and venue type.
12. **Waste/spillage** typically costed at 5–20% depending on method; garnish costed per-serve; batching improves yield and consistency.
13. **Dilution** varies by technique: roughly 20–25% for stirred and ~25%+ for shaken drinks, with Dave Arnold's *Liquid Intelligence* providing the authoritative scientific treatment and formulas.

## Details

### TRACK 1 — COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE AND POSITIONING

**Bar management & costing software.** The bar-operations software market is crowded but uniformly inventory-and-variance focused. **Backbar** offers free bar inventory management, a recipe builder, and menu costing, integrates with Toast and Square, and connects to POS data to calculate actual-vs-theoretical variance; pricing starts around $129/month for its Pro plan. **WISK** is AI-driven with Bluetooth-scale bottle weighing and 60+ POS integrations, positioned as a full-service (often hardware-inclusive) inventory platform with recipe costing and variance reporting. **Partender** is a mobile-first tool using smartphone-camera computer vision to estimate bottle levels for fast counts, but notably lacks POS integration and deep recipe costing. **BevSpot** (from ~$299/month/location) offers cloud inventory, purchasing, and recipe management. Others in the field include **Craftable**, **MarginEdge** (invoice/AP automation, ~$295–600/month), **AccuBar**, **Bar-i**, **MarketMan**, **Restaurant365**, and **Sculpture Hospitality**. Every one of these frames a "recipe" as a cost/inventory object for variance control — none treats a spec as a versioned creative artifact, and none has branching lineage or cross-creator forking.

**Cocktail reference & social apps.** **Difford's Guide** (launched 2004, run by Simon Difford) is the encyclopedic reference — highly organized, searchable, lets users enter their cabinet and find makeable drinks, save favorites, and even create their own recipes; criticisms center on sponsor-brand favoritism and product reviews perceived as "money driven." **Kindred Cocktails** is a crowd-sourced shared database with personal "Cocktail Books," submission/curation workflow, and explicit attribution style guidelines ("give credit where credit is due," creator format "Person, Bar, City," and authenticity flags: *Original creation / Authentic / Altered recipe"). It is the closest philosophical cousin — it even tracks "riffs" via its "Altered recipe" flag — but has no visual lineage graph, no forking mechanic, and no costing. **Untappd** is the social check-in giant — since its founding in 2010 the app has received over 1.5 billion check-ins, and Untappd for Business is used by nearly 20,000 venues in 75 countries — and recently expanded from beer to spirits, wine, and canned cocktails; its business-facing side draws complaints about menu-display reliability, forced logins/crashes, extra fees to add non-beer items, and template rigidity. **TheCocktailDB** is an open crowd-sourced API. None of these offers version-control or forking.

**"GitHub-for-X" precedents in recipes.** Multiple attempts exist but none succeeded as a mainstream bartender product: **Forkful** ("Cook. Modify. Repeat." — version control + forking of recipes, Postgres-backed), **dishout.recipes** (GitHub-repo-backed recipe viewer with fork-friendly JSON/YAML), **Recipe Commons** (Markdown recipes in GitHub under Creative Commons, explicitly "fork someone else's cookbook"), and the **nextcloud/cookbook** "Ability to version recipes" feature request (#364) — evidence of genuine latent demand for recipe versioning. The lesson: the *concept* recurs but always as developer-centric tooling requiring Git literacy; the opportunity for Proof is to deliver the fork-lineage mental model through a bartender-native visual canvas rather than Git mechanics.

**IP law and community norms.** The legal position is consistent across authorities: a drink recipe (a list of ingredients) cannot be copyrighted because recipes are considered "facts," per intellectual-property discussions at Tales of the Cocktail (moderated by spirits author and trademark attorney Philip Greene) and reporting in SevenFifty Daily, Fast Company, and The Daily Beast. Protectable elements are limited to **trademarks** on names (e.g., Gosling's "Dark 'n Stormy," Pusser's "Painkiller"), **copyright** on accompanying prose/photos, and **trade secrets** (undisclosed formulas, e.g., Bénédictine, Chartreuse). Notably, the Pusser's cease-and-desist against the NY bar "Painkiller" (which became PKNY) generated significant ill will — a cautionary tale about heavy-handed IP enforcement in this community. The bartending culture strongly favors open sharing with verbal/written credit: modern classics like Sam Ross's **Penicillin** (created 2005 at Milk & Honey) and **Paper Plane** (created 2007 for Chicago's The Violet Hour) are celebrated precisely *because* they are widely riffed and attributed — Ross has said "people would start to send me...photos of menus all over the world with my drinks on them...I get such a thrill out of that." This riff-with-credit norm is the exact cultural behavior Proof's fork-lineage + CC-BY model formalizes. **CC-BY for recipes** has real precedent: Foodista built a whole CC-BY cooking encyclopedia, and Creative Commons itself has endorsed CC-BY as a sound choice for recipes (since the recipe is a fact but its *expression* can be licensed).

### TRACK 2 — TECHNICAL BEST PRACTICES

**React Flow performance.** The official React Flow performance guide and independent audits (Synergy Codes, based on a 100-node MaterialUI test project) converge on the same rules: (1) **memoize custom node/edge components** with `React.memo` or declare them outside the parent, and memoize handler props with `useCallback` — the docs explicitly note you should memoize node components to avoid unnecessary re-renders; (2) **never subscribe to the whole nodes array** in a component that only needs a slice — store derived data (e.g., `selectedNodeIds`) as separate state; (3) use **Zustand with `useShallow`** (or `createWithEqualityFn` + shallow comparator, or memoized selectors via Reselect) — React Flow uses Zustand internally, so it's the natural choice and the docs recommend moving volatile node/edge state out of `useState`/Context into Zustand; (4) enable **`onlyRenderVisibleElements`** for virtualization of large graphs; (5) toggle node `hidden` to lazily expand deep trees; (6) simplify node CSS (avoid heavy shadows/gradients/animations). A DEV Community write-up corroborates that memoizing the node component was the single biggest FPS win, and that heavy per-node calculation should be deferred (`useDeferredValue`/off-main-thread) to avoid layout jank.

**Supabase/Postgres patterns.** RLS is the foundation: enable it on every table in the public schema (Supabase maps requests to `anon`/`authenticated` roles; enabling RLS defaults to deny-all until policies exist). Production guidance (MakerKit's 100+ deployments, DEV Community practitioners) stresses: use `SECURITY DEFINER` helper functions for recursive permission checks to avoid infinite-recursion 500 errors and costly per-row subqueries; store roles in JWT `app_metadata` to avoid per-row role-table lookups; UPDATE policies require a matching SELECT policy or updates silently fail; never expose the `service_role` key client-side. For the **public/private content split**, the natural pattern is a `status`/visibility column with policies allowing owner-only read on drafts and public read on published rows.

*Fork lineage (confirmed against PostgreSQL official docs):* Model each spec with a self-referencing `parent_id` (adjacency list). Traverse with `WITH RECURSIVE` — the Postgres docs state "Recursive queries are typically used to deal with hierarchical or tree-structured data" and the general form is "a non-recursive term, then UNION (or UNION ALL), then a recursive term." Walk **ancestors** by joining `s.id = a.parent_id`; find **descendants** by joining `s.parent_id = d.id`. Because forks cross creators and could theoretically loop, use PG15's native `SEARCH DEPTH FIRST` / `CYCLE ... SET ... USING path` clauses (added in PG14) or an `ARRAY[id]` visited-path guard; the docs warn the `LIMIT`-to-stop-loops trick is "not recommended" in production. Practitioner consensus (Leonard Marcq; Ackee) is to **default to adjacency list + recursive CTE** ("a must in almost all cases"; "always start with the adjacency list solution since it is simple, space-efficient") rather than `ltree`/materialized-path, which is only worth it for deep, read-mostly, rarely-moved trees and forces rewriting all descendant paths on any move. **Critical Supabase interaction:** the lineage-traversal function must run with policies (or `SECURITY DEFINER` with careful checks) that permit reading ancestor/descendant rows owned by *other* users, or public lineage will be silently truncated to the caller's own rows.

Ancestor traversal pattern:
```sql
WITH RECURSIVE ancestors AS (
    SELECT id, parent_id, name, created_by, 0 AS depth
    FROM specs WHERE id = :spec_id
  UNION ALL
    SELECT s.id, s.parent_id, s.name, s.created_by, a.depth + 1
    FROM specs s
    INNER JOIN ancestors a ON s.id = a.parent_id
)
SELECT * FROM ancestors;
```

*Immutable versioning (confirmed):* Postgres has no built-in system-versioned tables, and the `temporal_tables` extension is **not supported on managed platforms including Supabase** — so implement versioning manually, which suits Proof because "publish" is a discrete user action. Recommended design: a mutable `specs` table (evolving `draft_data` JSONB) plus an **append-only `spec_versions` table** storing **full immutable JSONB snapshots** with a `version_number` and `created_at`. Enforce immutability with a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger that raises an exception (append-only). Forks set `parent_version_id` to an exact `spec_versions.id`, so a fork always references a frozen snapshot even if the parent's draft later changes. Prefer **full snapshots over diffs** because specs are tiny and full copies make fork references trivially resolvable without replaying a diff chain (Postgres' own MVCC is an append-only full-copy model, per CMU's Andy Pavlo). SCD Type-2 `valid_from`/`valid_to` is the alternative only if point-in-time history queries become a requirement later.

*Full-text search (confirmed against Supabase docs):* Add a generated `tsvector` column and a GIN index, e.g. `add column fts tsvector generated always as (to_tsvector('english', name || ' ' || description)) stored;` then `create index ... using gin (fts);`. Use `websearch_to_tsquery` for user-facing search boxes (supports quotes, "or", negation). Use `setweight` to rank name ('A') above ingredients ('B'). **Important:** generated columns cannot pull from related tables — if ingredients live in a separate table, use a `BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE` trigger aggregating ingredient names via `string_agg`. From the client, use `supabase.textSearch('fts', query, { type: 'websearch', config: 'english' })`, and wrap `ts_rank` ordering in an RPC function.

**Radial/pie menu UX.** Research spanning Kurtenbach & Buxton's marking-menu work through Nielsen Norman Group and Big Medium establishes: radial menus are faster than linear lists for pointer/touch selection and get faster with practice via muscle memory (a 1988 study found radial faster than linear for eight-item lists; marking-menu field study found marks ~3.5× faster than menu selection). But a 2010 study (Ahlström et al.) found pie menus are *harder to learn* than linear/rectangular menus, so the movement-time advantage only materializes with familiarity — reserve them for high-frequency actions. Touch best practices: anchor the menu on the touched object (a fixed anchor reduces errors), trigger via long-press on canvas/objects, cap at **6–8 slices** for accuracy (per USPTO radial-menu patents), account for the finger obscuring part of the menu (partial-arc/offset designs), and provide accessible alternatives (keyboard, submenus). This directly fits Proof's "long-press a node to riff/branch" interaction behind the bar.

**Glassmorphism / Liquid Glass (2025–2026).** Glassmorphism is pervasive in 2025, and Apple's **Liquid Glass** (introduced at WWDC 2025, shipping across iOS 26 / iPadOS 26 / macOS Tahoe 26) extends it with real-time refraction/lensing. On the web it relies on CSS `backdrop-filter`, which is **GPU-accelerated and expensive** — costs compound with multiple blurred elements and during scroll/animation, and heavy use causes frame drops on low-power devices. Accessibility is the bigger risk: contrast/legibility can fall below WCAG 2.2 AA over busy backgrounds, and beta users reported menu-legibility problems and "distracting glossy" navigation. Best practice consensus (LogRocket, Setproduct, multiple UX sources): use glass **only on small floating chrome** (toolbars, modals, nav, key CTAs) — never full-screen or behind body text; keep blur modest (roughly 8–15px); ensure high-contrast neutral text with optional text shadows; respect **reduce-transparency / reduce-motion** OS settings; feature-detect and provide **solid fallbacks**; note SVG-filter refraction works only in Chromium (not Safari/Firefox). For a touch-first behind-the-bar tool, this argues for restrained glass accents over content sitting on solid, legible surfaces.

**Precise money handling.** JavaScript's single `number` type is IEEE-754 float (`0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004`), unsafe for money. Use **`decimal.js`** (ensure *every* operand is a `Decimal`, control rounding with `toDecimalPlaces`/rounding modes) and store as Postgres **`numeric`/`decimal`** (identical types, arbitrary precision). Critically, the `pg` Node driver **returns `numeric`/`decimal`/`bigint`/`money` as strings** to avoid precision loss — do not wrap them in `Number()`/`parseFloat`. Order operations to avoid sub-cent truncation (multiply before divide). An integer-minor-units (store cents) approach is a lighter alternative for single-currency apps.

### TRACK 3 — UK ON-TRADE COSTING VALIDATION

**GP% vs pour cost, and the VAT mechanics.** The UK on-trade uses **Gross Profit percentage** as the primary metric, whereas US bars use **pour cost %** (they are complements: Pour Cost % + GP% = 100%). Crucially, UK GP is calculated on the **ex-VAT net selling price**. Multiple UK trade sources make this explicit: Franklin & Sons walks through a Mojito example — net selling price £7.08, net cost £1.38, giving 80% GP — and Inn Express's cocktail GP calculator states "Sale prices are inclusive of VAT. GP calculated on ex-VAT amount." So the app must: take the menu price (inc-VAT), divide by 1.20 to get the net ex-VAT price, subtract net ingredient cost, and express GP as (net price − net cost) / net price. VAT is the standard **20%** rate; the pandemic-era reduced hospitality rates expired (the 5% rate ran 15 July 2020–30 Sep 2021, then 12.5% until 31 March 2022) and, per GOV.UK, "From 1 April 2022 the normal VAT rules apply, and VAT should be charged at the standard rate." The standard rate remains 20% through 2025–2026 with no announced change (confirmed by GOV.UK, House of Commons Library, and UK VAT guides).

**Wet GP benchmarks (sources disagree — flag this).** There is genuine spread. Consumer/tool sources cite higher targets: grossprofitcalculator.com says venues "aim for a wet GP (drinks) of around 70% to 75%," and Franklin & Sons says "On average you want a Gross Profit of around 70%." Cocktail-specific pricing often targets even higher (a well-made cocktail can hit ~75–80% GP). However, **actual achieved** wet GP is lower: the BBPA "Running a Pub" benchmarking survey (per Morning Advertiser) found "food margins range from 56.3% to 60.6%, compared to drinks margins ranging from 49.3% to 53.8%," with the highest GP achieved by rural destination pubs (56.3%) and food-led pubs (55.6%); accountancy discussion (AccountingWEB) reports real pub GP% "anywhere between 52 and 58%," with cocktail bars/lounges reaching ~62%. **Interpretation:** target cocktail GP of ~70–80% is a *pricing goal*, but real-world blended wet GP lands lower after waste, discounts, and mix — the app should let users set a target GP while showing the realized figure. Note also the important accounting distinction (which the app should respect): GP excludes staff wages; deducting wages yields net profit, not GP.

**Cocktail costing methodology.** US-style **pour cost targets** are commonly 18–24% for cocktail programs (higher-end craft bars 22–26%, high-volume/nightclub 15–18%), per Sculpture Hospitality, DishCost, and SommGeo. Costing must include *all* components, not just base spirit — non-spirit ingredients (juices, syrups, liqueurs, garnish) can be 30–50% of recipe cost (The Double Strainer). **Bottle yield:** a 750ml bottle is 25.4oz theoretically but realistically yields ~24–25oz in a well-run bar (5–8% loss to spillage/over-pour/residual), and using theoretical yield understates pour cost by ~1 percentage point. **Garnish** is costed per-serve (roughly $0.10–$0.75 depending on item; a bar doing 200 cocktails/night at $0.15 average spends ~$900/month on garnish alone). **Waste/spillage allowances:** cocktail-bar liquor/mixer waste is generally cited at 5–10%, while some pricing methods add a 15–20% waste-and-shrinkage charge. **Batching economics:** batching improves portion consistency and yield, speeds service, and cuts variance; citrus/syrups/liqueurs don't scale linearly, so batches must be tested, and "super juice" (acid-adjusted from peels) stretches one lime into ~8 equivalents.

**Dilution factors.** Water dilution from ice is a real ingredient (affecting both volume and, for costing batches, yield). Published figures: shaken drinks ~25–40% dilution (commonly ~25%+), stirred drinks ~20–25%; pre-batch guidance often uses ~25–30% water, with some adding 40–45% for spirit-forward stirred drinks and ~20% for sparkling. The authoritative source is **Dave Arnold's *Liquid Intelligence: The Art and Science of the Perfect Cocktail*** (2014), which treats dilution as the primary target of chilling (not a byproduct) and provides "Cocktail Calculus" formulas — e.g., a stirred-drink dilution formula expressed as a function of ABV — plus the weigh-before-and-after method for measuring exact dilution when pre-batching. The app's batching feature should let users apply a dilution % (or Arnold's method) so pre-diluted batches taste correct without individual mixing.

**UK hospitality economic context (2025–2026).** Standard VAT remains 20% on hospitality; the VAT registration threshold is £90,000 (from April 2024). There is active lobbying (UKHospitality) for a 10% hospitality rate — its campaign argues "In the UK, you pay one of the highest VAT rates in Europe. 20%...In France, Spain and Italy, it's 10%...In Germany? 7%," citing a YouGov poll that "79% of the public were in favour of a reduced rate," and HMRC estimated a cut to 10% on accommodation and F&B would cost ~£10.5bn in 2026/27 — but no cut has been enacted for general hospitality. Cost pressures cited for 2025–2026 include the April 2025 employer NIC changes (per GOV.UK, the secondary Class 1 NIC rate rose "from 13.8% to 15%" effective 6 April 2025, with the Secondary Threshold cut to "£5,000 a year...in effect from 6 April 2025 until 5 April 2028") and energy costs, squeezing net margins and making per-drink GP discipline more important. This context strengthens Proof's costing value proposition: tighter margins mean bartenders need fast, accurate per-spec GP more than ever.

## Recommendations

**For VISION.md — positioning:**
1. Position Proof explicitly in the *empty* quadrant: "creation-first + open/social," distinct from operations-first tools (Backbar/WISK) and flat reference/social apps (Difford's/Untappd/Kindred). Lead with the fork-lineage canvas as the wedge — it is the one thing no competitor has.
2. Frame the CC-BY commons as *formalizing an existing culture* (riff-with-credit), citing the Penicillin/Paper Plane riff ecosystem. Explicitly avoid IP overreach (the Pusser's/Painkiller cautionary tale) — Proof preserves *attribution*, not *ownership/exclusivity*.
3. Make **UK GP costing a first-class differentiator**, not a bolt-on. Competitors' costing is inventory-driven; Proof's is creation-driven and ex-VAT/GP-native.

**For CLAUDE.md — engineering standards (staged):**
4. **Non-negotiables from day one:** RLS enabled deny-by-default on every public-schema table; `decimal.js` + Postgres `numeric` for all money (never floats; never `Number()` a numeric string from `pg`); memoized React Flow nodes + Zustand `useShallow` selectors as the mandated state pattern.
5. **Lineage & versioning schema:** adjacency-list `parent_id` + recursive CTEs (PG15 `SEARCH`/`CYCLE`); append-only `spec_versions` table of full immutable JSONB snapshots with immutability trigger; forks reference exact `parent_version_id`. Write the lineage traversal as a `SECURITY DEFINER` RPC that can read across creators (or public lineage will truncate).
6. **Search:** generated `tsvector` column + GIN index + `websearch_to_tsquery`; use a trigger (not a generated column) if ingredients live in a child table.
7. **UI guardrails:** glassmorphism only on small floating chrome with solid fallbacks, modest blur, reduce-transparency support; radial menus capped at 6–8 slices, object-anchored, long-press-triggered, with keyboard/accessible fallbacks and onboarding hints (they're powerful but hard to learn).

**For PLAN.md — phasing:**
8. **Phase 1 (private R&D core):** React Flow lineage canvas + spec model + ex-VAT GP costing engine (per-ingredient pricing, selectable GP formulas, waste %, garnish per-serve, dilution %). This is usable solo and validates the hardest UX (canvas + costing) before any social features.
9. **Phase 2 (publishing + commons):** publish-to-snapshot flow, CC-BY licensing, public read RLS, full-text search, public spec pages with attribution.
10. **Phase 3 (cross-creator forking + social):** fork mechanic preserving cross-creator lineage, lineage visualization on public specs, follows/check-in-style social layer (Untappd-inspired but spec-centric).
11. **Benchmarks that change the plan:** if canvas FPS drops below ~60 at target graph sizes during Phase 1, prioritize virtualization/`onlyRenderVisibleElements` and node-simplification before adding features; if user testing shows radial menus confuse new users, add a conventional context-menu fallback before launch; if UK users report GP confusion, surface the ex-VAT calculation transparently (show net price derivation).

## Caveats
- **Benchmark disagreement on wet GP is real and unresolved:** pricing-target sources say 70–80% for cocktails; measured-outcome sources (BBPA drinks margins 49.3%–53.8%, accountancy ~52–62%) say lower for pubs/bars. The app should treat GP target as user-set and always show realized GP; do not hard-code a single "correct" number.
- **Software comparison sources skew commercial.** Several competitor rankings (ZipDo, Gitnux, WifiTalents) are affiliate/SEO listicles and some vendor pages (Backbar, WISK) are self-promotional; feature claims should be verified against primary product docs before being cited in marketing.
- **Dilution percentages vary by source and are technique/ice/skill-dependent;** treat the ranges as defaults, and lean on Arnold's weigh-before-and-after method for precision batching rather than fixed percentages.
- **VAT policy is in flux as a political topic** (active lobbying for 10%; a temporary 5% rate on certain children-focused and family leisure activities was announced for 2026, though this does not cover cocktails) — the model should make the VAT rate a configurable field rather than a hard-coded 20%, even though 20% is correct for cocktails today.
- **Legal note:** "recipes aren't copyrightable" is well-established for ingredient lists, but accompanying prose/photos *are* copyrightable and names *can* be trademarked — the commons T&Cs should cover user-contributed prose/images (which CC-BY can license) separately from the un-copyrightable spec itself.
- Some technical guidance comes from practitioner blogs (Synergy Codes, MakerKit, DEV Community); these were cross-checked against official React Flow, Supabase, and PostgreSQL documentation where possible, and the Postgres/Supabase lineage, versioning, and full-text-search patterns were verified against primary docs.
